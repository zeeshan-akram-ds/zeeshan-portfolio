Case studies / E-commerce delivery risk and churn

Late deliveries hurt reviews, and over 96% of customers never ordered again

Olist churn investigation. Public Kaggle dataset: 113,425 Brazilian marketplace orders. Python, MySQL and Power BI.

By Zeeshan Akram, founder of MarginLens.

The question

Revenue at this marketplace stopped growing in mid-2018. This investigation looks at why, through two questions: how much does delivery performance affect customer satisfaction, and how many customers come back after a first order.

The numbers

  • Average review score for late orders, against 4.2 for on-time ordersObserved in the data2.5
  • Customers who never placed a second orderObserved in the data96%+
  • November 2017 customers who bought again the next month (40 of 7,060)Observed in the data0.57%

Observed figures are measured directly in the dataset. Modeled figures are what-ifs built from it, not savings or forecasts.

Findings

  • On-time orders average 4.2 stars, late orders 2.5, and orders that failed to arrive 1.7.
  • Shipping cost also drives bad reviews. In some categories, such as Electronics, the shipping fee is sometimes about 70% of the item price, and customers left low scores even when the item arrived on time.
  • Some of the ten highest-earning sellers also have some of the worst late-delivery rates.
  • The dashboard shows delivery delays concentrated in the north of the country, with Amazonas and Roraima among the worst states.
  • Roughly 97% of customers are one-time buyers. In this data, on-time delivery and five-star reviews did not turn into repeat purchases.
  • Every customer segment averages about 4 stars or better, including customers who never came back. That points away from dissatisfaction as the main reason for the missing repeat orders.
  • Monthly revenue grew fast through 2017, peaking in November, then flattened to 1% or 2% monthly growth between March and August 2018.

What the data does and does not show

The data supports a link between late delivery and low review scores. It does not show that late delivery is why customers fail to come back: customers with on-time deliveries and good reviews also mostly did not reorder. Delivery and repeat purchase look like two separate problems, and this analysis treats them that way.

What the audit concluded

RecommendationFix late delivery to protect reviews, and treat repeat purchase as its own problem with its own test, such as an automatic second-purchase offer after a successful first delivery.

Methodology

  • Cleaning and exploration: Python, merging five relational tables, then exploring review scores against delivery time and freight against price.
  • Business queries: five SQL queries in MySQL using CTEs and window functions: month-over-month revenue growth, delivery against reviews, late-delivery rates for top sellers, RFM segmentation and cohort retention.
  • Segmentation: because about 97% of customers are one-time buyers, standard quintile RFM buckets would have been useless, so custom absolute thresholds were used to isolate the best customers.
  • Dashboard: a four-page Power BI report on a star schema (one fact table, three dimensions), covering performance, logistics, retention and an executive summary.

Limitations

  • Delivery performance is linked to review scores here. It is not shown to cause customers to stop buying. This is observational data with no experiment.
  • A customer who ordered once is counted as not returning, and the dataset covers a limited window, so some customers who would have returned later are counted as lost.
  • Olist is a marketplace with many third-party sellers, not a single Shopify store. Seller and shipping mix will differ for an individual brand.
  • The dataset is public, not client data.
